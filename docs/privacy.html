LabelTruth

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 31 July 2026

The short version. LabelTruth has no accounts and no analytics. We do not collect, store, or share any personal information about you. Photos from your camera are analysed on your phone and are never uploaded. The only thing the app ever sends over the internet is a barcode number you chose to scan.

Who we are

LabelTruth is an Android app published by Pitch Code. If you have a question about privacy, contact labeltruth.support@gmail.com.

What the app does

LabelTruth scans a food product's barcode, or reads a printed ingredient list with your camera, and explains what each ingredient is using published information from food-safety regulators.

Information we do not collect

To be explicit, LabelTruth does not collect any of the following:

  • Your name, email address, phone number, or postal address
  • Any account or login — the app has no sign-up at all
  • Your location
  • Advertising identifiers, or any other device or tracking identifier
  • Analytics, usage statistics, crash telemetry, or behavioural profiling
  • Your contacts, messages, files, or calendar

There are no advertisements in the app, and no third-party advertising or analytics SDKs.

Your camera

The app needs camera access to read barcodes and ingredient labels. Barcode detection and text recognition run entirely on your device using Google's ML Kit on-device libraries.

Camera images are held in memory only for as long as it takes to read them. They are never saved to storage and never transmitted anywhere.

Information stored on your device

Some information is saved on your phone so the app is useful. None of it is sent to us or to anyone else, and none of it leaves your device.

WhatWhyWhere
Your profile — allergens, dietary preferences, health considerations you choose to tick So a scan can flag what matters to you specifically On your device only
Scan history — product names and scores So you can look back at what you scanned On your device only
Ingredient reference data So the app works offline Bundled inside the app

Backup and device-to-device transfer are switched off for this app, so this information is not copied to cloud backups either.

The one network request the app makes

When you scan a barcode, the app sends that barcode number to Open Food Facts, a free and open food products database, in order to look up the product name and its ingredient list.

  • Only the barcode number is sent. No personal information accompanies it.
  • No photograph is ever sent.
  • The request identifies only the app and its version, not you.

Like any web request, Open Food Facts will see your device's IP address, which is how the internet works rather than something we transmit deliberately. Their handling of that is covered by the Open Food Facts privacy policy.

If you use only the label-scanning mode, the app makes no network requests at all and works completely offline.

Children's privacy

LabelTruth is not directed at children and collects no personal information from anyone, including children.

Deleting your data

Your profile and scan history live only on your phone. You can clear your scan history inside the app at any time, and uninstalling the app permanently removes everything. Because we never receive your data, there is nothing for us to delete on our side and no request you need to send us.

Where the ingredient information comes from

Ingredient descriptions and safety assessments are drawn from published sources including the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA), the World Health Organization and its International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC), the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA), and EU regulations on food additives, allergen labelling and flavourings. Each ingredient entry in the app shows its own sources.

Product data comes from Open Food Facts and is used under the Open Database License (ODbL).

Not medical advice. LabelTruth is for information only. It is not medical advice and not a diagnosis, and its ratings cannot account for your individual medical history. Always check the physical packaging for allergen information, and speak to a doctor, dietitian, or pharmacist about your own health.

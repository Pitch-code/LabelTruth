Food labels are written to be skimmed, not understood

"Emulsifier (E471)". "Sulphite ammonia caramel". "Natural flavouring." Most of us put the packet in the trolley and hope for the best. LabelTruth reads the label for you and explains, in plain language, what each ingredient actually is and whether anyone credible has raised a concern about it.

How it works

  1. Scan the barcode

    Point your camera at the barcode and the product's ingredient list is looked up.

  2. Or photograph the label directly

    No barcode, or the product isn't in any database? Point the camera at the printed ingredient list instead. Text recognition runs on your phone, so this works on any package.

  3. Get a verdict you can interrogate

    A clear score, then every ingredient broken down individually — what it is, why it's in there, who should be careful, and the source for each claim.

What makes it different

Sources, not guesses

Every rating traces to a named published source — EFSA, the WHO and IARC, the US FDA, EU additive and allergen regulations. You can read them yourself.

Honest about gaps

If an ingredient isn't in the dictionary, the app says so plainly instead of inventing an answer.

Works offline

The ingredient dictionary ships inside the app. Label scanning needs no signal at all — useful in a supermarket basement.

Personal to you

Tick your allergens, diet and health considerations, and scans get checked against them. It stays on your phone.

No account, no tracking

No sign-up, no analytics, no ads, no advertising identifiers. Camera images never leave your device.

Colour-coded at a glance

Green through red, distinguishable by lightness as well as hue so it still works if you're colour-blind.

Privacy, plainly

LabelTruth collects nothing about you. There is no account to create. Camera frames are analysed on your phone and are never uploaded. Your profile and scan history stay on your device, and uninstalling removes them completely.

The only thing the app ever sends over the internet is a barcode number you chose to scan, so a product can be looked up. Full detail is in the privacy policy.

Not medical advice. LabelTruth is for information only. It is not medical advice and not a diagnosis, and it cannot account for your individual medical history. Always check the physical packaging for allergen information, and speak to a doctor, dietitian or pharmacist about your own health.

Coverage, honestly stated

No complete list of every food ingredient exists anywhere, and any app claiming otherwise is overstating. The dictionary is built from authoritative public sources and grows steadily. Where an ingredient isn't recognised yet, the app tells you rather than filling the gap with a guess.
